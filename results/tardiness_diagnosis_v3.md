# Why is DANIEL-T so far from CP-SAT? Tardiness diagnosis, benchmark v3

Protocol: `docs/tardiness_diagnosis_protocol_v3.md`. Generated by `tools/analyze_tardiness_diagnosis_v3.py` from `results/tardiness_diagnosis_v3/` (`tools/tardiness_diagnosis_v3.py`). No training was run. Evaluation split only; the reserved final-test sets were never loaded.

## 1. The reward: dense, not sparse

**Plain answer: the tardiness reward is dense.** It is not paid only when a late job finishes. At every step the environment estimates total tardiness from each job's *lower-bound* completion time, and the reward is minus the change in that estimate. This is the same construction as DANIEL's makespan reward, which is minus the change in the largest lower-bound completion time. So **Part B (a dense tardiness reward option) was skipped**: the reward it would add already exists.

The estimate (a potential) is total tardiness evaluated on each job's current completion-time lower bound:

`daniel/objectives.py:198-205`

```python
def tardiness_potential(op_ct_lb: np.ndarray, job_last_op_id: np.ndarray,
                        env_job_idx: np.ndarray, due_dates: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray:
    """Lower-bound tardiness: T evaluated on each job's current completion-time LOWER BOUND.

    Used as a shaping potential. Once every operation is scheduled the lower bounds ARE the
    true completion times, so the potential equals T exactly at the end of the episode.
    """
    return total_tardiness(op_ct_lb[env_job_idx, job_last_op_id], due_dates)
```

At every step the environment takes the change in that estimate as the tardiness reward. The makespan potential is computed in the same place:

`daniel/fjsp_env_same_op_nums.py:665-680`

```python
        tardiness_score = self.compute_tardiness_score()
        tardiness_reward = self.tardiness_score - tardiness_score
        self.tardiness_score = tardiness_score

        if self.reward_version == "v3" and self.pref_weights is not None:
            mk_potential = np.max(self.op_ct_lb, axis=1)
            reward = self.reward_v2.step_reward_weighted(self.pref_weights, self.v2_mk_potential - mk_potential,
                                                         chosen_carbon, tardiness_reward, self.pt_upper_bound,
                                                         self.v2_mk_ref, self.v2_cb_ref)
            self.v2_mk_potential = mk_potential
        elif self.reward_version in ("v2", "v3"):
            mk_potential = np.max(self.op_ct_lb, axis=1)
            reward = self.reward_v2.step_reward(self.v2_mk_potential - mk_potential, chosen_carbon,
                                                tardiness_reward, self.pt_upper_bound,
                                                self.v2_mk_ref, self.v2_cb_ref)
            self.v2_mk_potential = mk_potential
```

Both potentials start from zero, so the episode's return is exactly minus the final value:

`daniel/fjsp_env_same_op_nums.py:256-261`

```python
        # Tardiness shaping starts from a potential of ZERO, not from the initial lower-bound
        # tardiness, so the first step also charges any tardiness already unavoidable at t=0.
        # The episode return is then exactly -T/time_scale rather than -T/time_scale + const.
        self.tardiness_score = np.zeros(self.number_of_envs)
        # v2 makespan potential also starts at zero (return = -score exactly; results/v2_fixes.md, A3)
        self.v2_mk_potential = np.zeros(self.number_of_envs)
```

With goal `t` (DANIEL-T), only the tardiness term is weighted (w_t = 1). It is converted back to raw time and divided by the size's Tref:

`daniel/objectives.py:118-127`

```python
    def step_reward(self, mk_potential_delta, chosen_carbon, tardiness_delta, u, mk_ref, cb_ref):
        w_m, w_c, w_t = self.weights
        reward = np.zeros_like(np.asarray(chosen_carbon, dtype=np.float64))
        if w_m:
            reward = reward + w_m * u * np.asarray(mk_potential_delta) / mk_ref
        if w_c:
            reward = reward - w_c * np.asarray(chosen_carbon) / cb_ref
        if w_t:
            reward = reward + w_t * u * np.asarray(tardiness_delta) / self.tbar
        return reward
```

**What the lower bound is.** Before any scheduling, each operation's lower-bound completion time is the sum of its job's minimum processing times so far. When an operation is scheduled, its job's later lower bounds move by the delay (`op_ct_lb[mask1] += diff`). The bound ignores waiting for machines until the job's own next operation is placed. The consequence: a decision that delays *another* job is charged to that other job only when it is scheduled. The reward is dense, but its credit for a decision can arrive later.

**Measured** along DANIEL-T s111's greedy schedules (the subset in section 4, replayed through the environment with goal `t`):

| Size | Steps per episode | Steps with a non-zero tardiness reward | Steps where a late job finishes (when a sparse reward would pay) | Steps with a non-zero makespan reward | Share of the tardiness return paid in the first half of the episode | Same, makespan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10x5 | 50 | 16% | 9% | 15% | 8% | 30% |
| 20x10 | 200 | 19% | 7% | 6% | 1% | 40% |

The tardiness reward is non-zero on two to three times as many steps as a sparse reward would be, and at least as often as the makespan reward. Almost all of it arrives in the second half of the episode, because the lower-bound estimate passes a due date only late in the schedule. The reward is dense in form, but it is back-loaded: the early decisions that cause lateness are paid for many steps later.

## 2. What the network sees about due dates

Due dates are divided by the fixed time scale 99, the same scale as processing times:

`daniel/fjsp_env_same_op_nums.py:505-505`

```python
        self.due_dates = due / self.pt_upper_bound
```

**Operation features (2 of 12).** These are given per operation, repeated for every operation of the same job:

`daniel/fjsp_env_same_op_nums.py:719-723`

```python
            est, work, _, _ = self.v2_job_state()
            slack = self.due_dates - est - work                                        # [E, J]
            time_to_due = self.due_dates - est
            features.append(np.take_along_axis(slack, self.v2_op_job, axis=1))
            features.append(np.take_along_axis(time_to_due, self.v2_op_job, axis=1))
```

| Feature | Definition (all divided by 99) | Normalisation |
|---|---|---|
| slack | d_j − EST_j − W_j: the due date minus the earliest start of the job's next operation minus the job's remaining minimum work | z-score per instance at every step, over the operations not yet finished (`norm_op_features`) |
| time to due | d_j − EST_j | the same z-score |

**Pair feature (1 of 10).** This is given per (job, machine) candidate pair:

`daniel/fjsp_env_same_op_nums.py:832-834`

```python
            late = np.maximum(0.0, pair_start + self.candidate_pt + (work - cand_min_pt)[:, :, None] - due)
            done_late = np.maximum(0.0, self.candidate_free_time[:, :, None] - due)
            late = np.where(finished[:, :, None], np.broadcast_to(done_late, late.shape), late)
```

| Feature | Definition (divided by 99) | Normalisation |
|---|---|---|
| lateness | max(0, start of the pair + its processing time + the job's remaining minimum work after it − d_j); for a finished job, its actual lateness | none: fed to the actor as is |

**Not seen:** the due date itself, the current time and total or current tardiness. The machine features carry no due-date information.

**One consequence of the z-score.** It removes the sign and scale of slack and time to due. After it, the network can tell which job has less slack than the others, but not whether any job is already late. Only the pair lateness feature keeps absolute units, and it is zero for every pair that is not yet estimated late.

## 4. Splitting the gap: the no-waiting rule against learning

**Instances:** the first 20 evaluation instances per size. Every one has a CP-SAT single-objective tardiness result.

**What "without waiting" means.** DANIEL's action mask allows only the pairs that can start at the earliest possible start time (`unavailable_pairs = pair_free_time > next_schedule_time`). So DANIEL can never leave a machine idle on purpose to keep it free for a more urgent job (a non-delay schedule). CP-SAT has no such restriction.

**(b) is found by a time-limited search.**
- **Method:** random-key genetic search over schedules decoded by the same mask, in the environment's own arithmetic. Every (operation, machine) pair has a key, and at each step the allowed pair with the smallest key is chosen. Every no-waiting schedule can be reached this way.
- **Settings:** population 200 per instance, 20 kept each generation, uniform crossover per operation, 5% of keys mutated by N(0, 0.1), 5% random newcomers, seed 2026.
- **Limit:** a fixed generation budget (below).
- **Seeds in the starting population:** the three rules, CP-SAT's own schedule order decoded through the mask, and DANIEL-T's committed greedy and best-of-100 schedules for all three seeds. The result is therefore never worse than DANIEL-T.
- **What (b) is:** an upper bound on the best no-waiting tardiness, not a proven optimum.

**Checks:** every schedule in the table was replayed through the real environment and re-scored by the independent scorer, and all agree. The search's decoder allows exactly the environment's pairs at every step (`tests/test_tardiness_diagnosis_v3.py`).

## Verdict

- **10x5:** of the greedy gap of 231.6, at most 15% is due to the no-waiting rule and at least 85% to learning. With best of 100 samples the shares are 36% and 64%.
- **20x10:** of the greedy gap of 1078.2, at most 58% is due to the no-waiting rule and at least 42% to learning. With best of 100 samples the shares are 72% and 28%.

**Short verdict.**

- **Sparse reward: ruled out.** The tardiness reward is already dense, built the same way as DANIEL's makespan reward. Part B was therefore not run. The reward is back-loaded, though: more than 90% of each episode's tardiness return arrives in the second half.
- **10x5: learning explains most of the gap.**
  - A schedule DANIEL's own decision rule can produce reaches a mean of 50.5 or better on these instances, against CP-SAT's 16.1.
  - DANIEL-T greedy reaches 247.7, so at least 85% of the greedy gap is the policy not finding schedules it is allowed to make.
  - Its best of 100 samples (111.2) still leaves at least 64% to learning.
- **20x10: both causes matter, and this test cannot fully separate them.**
  - The best no-waiting schedule found (728.3) is far above CP-SAT (100.0), so the no-waiting rule costs at most 628 of the 1,078 greedy gap (58%).
  - The search only improved DANIEL-T's own schedules by 8% (789.5 to 728.3), so the true best no-waiting value may be much lower. That would move more of the gap to learning.
- **Two signs that learning is weak at 20x10:**
  - DANIEL-T removes only 14% of the reference rule's tardiness (10x5: 57%).
  - It is beaten on tardiness by the carbon specialist (1206.9 against 1058.6, `results/baselines_v3.md`).
- **The no-waiting rule is costly for simple rules, not only for CP-SAT.**
  - The same EDD+ECT rule gives 932 with waiting allowed but 5,723 without, because without waiting a job is forced onto whichever eligible machine is free now, however slow.
  - CP-SAT's own job order, forced through the same mask, gives 2,766.

**Most of the gap:** at 10x5, learning. At 20x10, the no-waiting action mask accounts for at most about half, and the rest is learning.

Single search seed and the committed specialists only: numbers only, no significance claims.
